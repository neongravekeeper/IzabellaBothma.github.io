Others

The Poem

Others Up a long winding road and across the train tracks are society’s outcasts, But why are they other when we are them? Why are they thrown away when they are us? How can I, one of the other dream of fulfilling my life’s goals while they live in a compound, or sanctum of wasted potential Their histories reduced to nothing but a name and prescriptions among so many others

Breakdown

The first stanza is meant to make Weskoppies feel physically separated from the rest of society. These people aren’t really far away, but society has made them distant.

The core of my poem is to confront the relationship that we have with one another. How it is so easy to use words like “they”, othering people who do not conform to societal standards.

“How can I, one of the other”, may initially come off as strange, but I am placing myself within the category I am questioning. I have personally struggled with mental illness and have firsthand experience from within my family.

The line “compound, or sanctum” is intentionally ironic. It is a place where people, others, are confined, but for some it could be a place of comfort. Whether it is society being kept safe from them, or them being kept safe from society. It is intended to make the reader question different perspectives.

“wasted potential” is explicitly judgmental. Their lives could have been more, if not for societal ridicule and shunning. It is the entire community’s responsibly to support each other, regardless of differences.

The final stanza is meant to show how institutionalization can erase individuality. People who have history, personalities, hopes, dreams, and relationships become names in files with only their medical history to define them.
